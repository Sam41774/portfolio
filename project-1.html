Inverted Pendulum

The inverted pendulum is a classic control theory problem. In my project it consists of a pendulum balanced upside down by a cart driven by a motor.

ROLE Independent Project
TOOLS MatLab, Solidworks, C++
TIMELINE March — August 2026
STATUS Completed
Overview Approach Challenges Results

Overview

The inverted pendulum is a classic control theory problem: a pendulum is mounted upside down on a cart, and the cart must be moved to keep the pendulum balanced vertically. It's an unstable system, meaning without active correction it will simply fall.

I decided to initialy pursue this topic as an indepent final prjoect in MATH 2406H. The goal of this iniitial projcet was to simulate the system and create my own optimzation program to find the correct gains for the simulated PID controler. To do this I ultumatly derived the equations of motion for the pendlum and simulated it in MatLab. The full report is avalible here.

This initial project, that included indpeth anaylsis of the system, laied the groundwork for the physical project depicted here.

Approach

To control the pendulum I used a PD controler. I orginally intened to a use feedfowrad state space model, like in the initial simulations, but chose the PD contorler for simplicity.

  • Milestone 1: Built the Physical Rig

    • The rig is comprised of 2020 v-slot alluminium extrusion for main rail where the gantry rides, as well as the legs. I chose this becasue it offers extensive flexibilty while also offering rigidity and a built in track for the gantry.

    The gantry is moved via a belt that is connected to the stepper motor and the gantry itslef. The stepper motor I chose for this project is a standard Nema 17 stepper capable of 2.1A (part number: 17hs24-2104s) that I was able to scavange. I chose this stepper motor becasue it ensures that torque would not be a limiting factor.

    Finaly the pendulum mount itself consists of an optical encoder (this one), I chose an optical encoder because it is very presices and adds very little friction to the pendulum alowing the movement to be as smooth as possible.

  • Milestone 2: Developed the Motor Control Algorithim

    • To control the stepper motor I orginialy intented to use a simple quadruple half-H bridge driver, however due to the high current draw of the motor it expereinced overheating issues. To fix this I upgraded to a drv8825 stepper motor driver that was capable of supplying the full 2.1A.

    Developing a control algorthim for the motor to work with PD controler took a substanial amount of time becasue of two main reasons: the force applied to gantry is what is important and the stepper motor takes time to reach a specified speed without skiping steps.

    To remedy the issue of not being able to easily apply a specifed force to the gantry I simply focused on controlling the acceleration which is easier to calculate ussing the step infomration from the stepper motor.

    Scince the acceleration of the gantry is tracked by the step information from the motor, not having the stepper motor skip steps was imparitive. To acchive this I eventualy settled on using the AccelStepper library and a combination of hard coded limits to keep things as simple and reliable as posible.

  • Milestone 3: implemented and Tuned the Controller

    • Once the motor control was taken care of, implementing the PD controler was quite straight forward. The simulations from my earlier reserch helped substantialy in the tuning process by giving the relitive size of each gain to each other. However, because I ultimatly chnaged aproacehs and the simulation was lacking variablese, it was not entirly accurate.

Challenges

  • Steper Motor Control:

    • I orginialy chose a stepper motor for this project becasue I mistakanly thought the position of the gantry would be important. The pendulum is balcnaced only by calculating the force to be applied to the gantry, so it is not required to track the position. I discovered this while working on the earilier paper, but decided to use what I had on hand.

    This made it harder to program the controler, but it did allow the gantry to stay in the middle of the track with no additinal hardware, such as another encoder for the belt. Ultimatly, while this desicion proved helpful in reducing the total number of components and greatly expanding my knowledge of stepper motor control, a standard motor would be better suited for this project.

    Results

    The pendulum is very stable and it can balance indefintly. It can correct disturbances in less then 5 seconds.
